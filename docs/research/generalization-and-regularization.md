# Generalization and regularization for Secret Wards

The current detector at `d8973ab` uses deterministic regex and format adapters. It has no trained weights. The original-corpus result measures whether 7,917 source-present labeled identifier surfaces remained visible after release. Zero were observed. This is complete observed coverage on those labels and that corpus. It leaves generalization, precision and privacy outside the measured scope. The original templates informed rule development. The newer open benchmarks show that the same detector relies on familiar schemas and labels. ## What clinical transfer studies support

- Training and testing in one institution can hide a large transfer gap. Yang et al. reported strict F1 of 0.9547 in the source setting and 0.8568 after transfer to UF Health in the published journal study. A multi-institution study also found sensitivity varied by note and entity type. For our synthetic system, hold out full institutions and template families and report enough support for each slice. The external figures describe those studies' models and corpora. [Cross-institute study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12911-019-0935-4) · [Neural transferability study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8633667/)
- An Indian discharge-summary study found substantially weaker transfer from a US-only training arm and reported gains with local and synthetic adaptation. The best locally adapted synthetic arm reached 0.98 micro-F1 on 20 held-out summaries from one institution (Tables 6–7). That supports building independent local layout, language and role variation. Performance on wider Indian sources and scripts remains a separate question. The study's models and collection procedure are research evidence. The organizer's eligible run is offline, CPU-only and synthetic-only. [Indian discharge-summary study](https://aclanthology.org/2024.bionlp-1.26/) · [Organizer constraints](../PROBLEM_STATEMENT.md#7-ground-rules)
- A synthetic-to-real drift study found that drift indicators and out-of-distribution F1 were associated, and that its synthetic-mixture results depended on the proportion and identifier category. Use per-category distribution summaries to target collection. Within the team dataset, compare broader forms against making more records from the same forms at equal sample and review budgets. A synthetic benchmark cannot establish real-world validity. [Measuring the gap](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13130710/)
- Downstream utility needs its own outcome. Extract exact dose, unit, frequency, negation, lab and temporal facts before and after the release. Publish results over all input and over released records. An earlier clinical evaluation used medication-name extraction as an independent clinical use case. More recent clinician review found clinically consequential edits automated measures missed. [Clinical information extraction evaluation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3555323/) · [Clinical utility evaluation](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1637/)

## Dropout and other learned regularizers

Primary-source searches did not establish a training technique called “COPA dropout” for this task. The closest common phrase is dropout preventing **co-adaptation** of neural units. COPA is also the name of a causal-reasoning benchmark. The similarly named CoPA paper studies correlation-shift adaptation in vision and describes input knockout in its GPA extension. Those uses give no clinical NER evidence for a method under the requested name. [Co-adaptation paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.0580) · [Dropout paper](https://jmlr.org/papers/v15/srivastava14a.html) · [COPA benchmark](https://asgordon.github.io/copa.html) · [CoPA and GPA paper](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/papers/11855.pdf)

| Option | Where it applies | Proposed comparison and priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stable feature families and L2 | Small linear-chain CRF or token classifier. Penalizes large weights on sparse shortcuts and leaves inference simple. | First learned baseline once labels are ready. Keep tokenization fixed. Use character n-grams, shape, script, neighboring words and source-derived field roles. Compare a small L2 grid against weak regularization on grouped validation. [CRFsuite manual](https://www.chokkan.org/software/crfsuite/manual.html#lbfgs) |
| L1 and vocabulary pruning | Sparse model weights and rare lexical features. Can reduce reliance on uniquely identifying strings and reduce model size. Weak signals for sparse classes can also disappear. | Compare against the selected L2 baseline on held-out formats and rare entity recall, controlling feature inventory and train groups. [CRFsuite manual](https://www.chokkan.org/software/crfsuite/manual.html#lbfgs) |
| Feature masking and targeted augmentation | Train-time masking of optional lexical, gazetteer or alias features while retaining characters, labels, offsets, role context and clinical values. | Ablate one feature group at a time. Start with deterministic cue perturbations whose expected labels are reviewed. Random feature masking has an analogy in noisy-text NER, and frequency-aware feature penalties have separate evidence. Neither establishes a clinical gain here. [Noisy NER feature dropout](https://aclanthology.org/W16-3927/) · [Feature-dependent CRF regularization](https://aclanthology.org/N16-1032/) |
| Early stopping and ordinary dropout | Small neural sequence tagger. Select an epoch on grouped validation. During training mask embedding or activation units. | Consider a character CNN or small BiLSTM-CRF only if the classical comparator exposes a recurring error with contextual evidence. Compare validation-selected early stopping alone and with ordinary dropout, using the same architecture and training data. [BiLSTM-CRF NER](https://aclanthology.org/N16-1030/) · [Dropout paper](https://jmlr.org/papers/v15/srivastava14a.html) |
| Locked dropout and R-Drop | Locked dropout shares a mask across sequence positions. R-Drop uses two stochastic training passes and penalizes disagreement between their predictions. | Test locked dropout after ordinary dropout if a recurrent model is justified. Defer R-Drop unless instability remains and the extra CPU training cost fits. The original R-Drop results are from other tasks. A sequence tagger would need a defined sequence or token-marginal consistency objective. [Recurrent dropout](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.05287) · [R-Drop](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/5a66b9200f29ac3fa0ae244cc2a51b39-Abstract.html) |
| Group balancing and calibration | Underrepresented scripts, roles or institutions for trained models. Calibration governs thresholds and abstention at inference. | Compare simple group-balanced sampling before a more complex group-DRO objective. Fit calibrators only on separate grouped validation support and check them again under shift. Regex scores remain heuristic rankings. [Group DRO](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ryxGuJrFvS) · [Calibration under shift](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/8558cb408c1d76621371888657d2eb1d-Abstract.html) |

## Proposed experiment order

1. **Make evaluation reliable.** Developer B reproduces the same-surface role reversal, inferred truth label and missing-artifact cases with regression tests. Keep exact typed spans, one-to-one same-type overlaps, role-aware release outcomes, coverage and incomplete scans separate. For supervised metrics, use independently adjudicated units and require exhaustive annotation for error denominators that treat absent labels as negatives. Use open results for debugging. Keep the sealed cohort private. [Neutral detector inputs](https://github.com/baymax-afk/scaler_hackthon/issues/21) · [Role-aware release scoring](https://github.com/baymax-afk/scaler_hackthon/issues/22) · [Release reconciliation](https://github.com/baymax-afk/scaler_hackthon/issues/23)
2. **Target measured runtime failures.** Developer A compares the existing rules with source-derived field and resource roles, normalized text with original-span mapping, and edits bounded by clinical context. Use paired alias substitutions, field reordering, Unicode whitespace or canonical forms, and clinician/relative minimal pairs. Run each change independently and together. Broad whole-field edits on uncertain labels need protected clinical checks. Count files withheld and retain the FHIR date guard while evaluating structure-safe date options. [Prior detector research](robustness-techniques.md#techniques-to-qualify)
3. **Measure whether diversity matters at fixed size.** Developer B compares four equal-count, equal-review-budget arms that test familiar-template sampling, independently authored layouts, adjudicated development hard cases, and a mix of layout changes and hard cases. For hard cases, sample false positives, near misses and ordinary controls. Balance format, entity and script support. Group by patient, institution, template and near-duplicate ancestry before augmentation. Compare new-family generalization with fixed tuning effort. Report all supported slices and grouped uncertainty intervals where enough independent groups exist. Use controlled perturbation examples to diagnose whether diversity helped structure, surface forms or both. [Dataset design](dataset-v2-design.md) · [CheckList](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.442/) · [Distribution shift benchmark](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/koh21a.html) · [Clinical active learning](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6568071/)
4. **Decide whether learned regularization is justified.** After adjudication, freeze a common evaluation and compare the revised deterministic baseline with a small independent learned detector. Hold model capacity, features, tokenization, seeds and train groups fixed across regularization arms. Select hyperparameters by grouped cross-validation within development. Reserve the separate validation split for setting thresholds and checking calibration. Keep the 600-record final evaluation untouched and publish the frozen revisions. Measure exact typed precision/recall/F1, worst supported script and role slices, clinical preservation, release completeness and p50/p95 latency, training cost and peak RSS under the 2-CPU and 4-GB caps. Use clinical preservation and export coverage as acceptance gates. Evaluate a combined detector only after the standalone comparisons. [Evaluation and architecture](version-2-improvement-plan.md#comparisons-and-adoption) · [Organizer execution limits](../SPEC.md#2-execution-environment)

The measured results support prioritizing evaluation and deterministic generalization. The learned comparisons above are hypotheses, with no trained models or measured regularization gains in this study.
